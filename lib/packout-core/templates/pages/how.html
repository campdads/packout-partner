{{partner}} looks after places that need attention. Here is how the work goes, from the first report to the record of what was done.

1. Someone reports a Spot

A Spot is a place that needs attention: litter at a trailhead, a dumped load by a road, a fire ring full of cans. Anyone can report one, with a photo and a pin on the map. A coordinator looks at each report before it is shown, and can show a sensitive place at an approximate point.

2. An organization plans a mission

Organizations and their teams choose Spots, set a day and a place to meet, and invite people to sign up. A campaign gathers several missions toward one goal.

3. The crew cleans it and records the work

After the cleanup the crew records how many people came, for how long, and what they carried out, with photos. Weights that were measured are kept apart from estimates, and a coordinator checks a record before it is counted.

4. The place is watched

A cleaned Spot stays on the map. People who pass by later say whether it is still clean or needs another visit.
